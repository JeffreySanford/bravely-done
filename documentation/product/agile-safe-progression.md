# Progressive Agile and SAFe Structure

Teaching real, transferable Agile/SAFe practice — not just team-level Scrum — is a core product goal,
not a late-stage "complex work" add-on. The game scales the same way real SAFe scales: individual →
team (Program/ART-equivalent) → community (Portfolio-equivalent), and Ember teaches the methodology
explicitly at the team/community tier.

## Vocabulary: dual-labeled by default

Every PM/Agile concept is shown in **both** its game term and its real-world term together (e.g.
"Facilitator — aka Scrum Master") **by default**, not hidden behind a "professional mode" toggle. This
is deliberate: players should absorb transferable real PM vocabulary while playing in the game's own
vocabulary, not have real terminology optionally bolted on later.

- Task → Quest (aka Story/Task)
- Subtask → Encounter
- Project → Questline (aka Epic)
- Milestone → Landmark
- Sprint → Expedition/Adventure Sprint (aka Sprint)
- Initiative → Campaign (aka Feature/Capability)
- Team → Party (aka Team/ART)
- Organization → Guild (aka Program/Portfolio)

## Complexity trigger

A simple quest stays simple. The application may suggest a questline when work has multiple outcomes,
dependencies, owners, risks, or spans several sessions.

## Role/skill-tree system (team tier)

Players assume PM roles — Facilitator (aka Scrum Master), Product lead (aka PM), Adventurer (aka Dev),
Scout (aka Tester), and others — each with its **own full branching skill tree**, D&D-class-style, not
a simple linear perk list. This is a mechanical analogy only; Ember's and the avatars' *visual* language
stays stylized sci-fi/cyber (see [AI companion](ai-companion.md)), not fantasy.

- Each role levels **independently** (e.g. a player can be level 6 Facilitator, level 9 Adventurer, and
  level 5 Scout simultaneously).
- Roles are **switchable** at will.
- Framing is **deliberately domain-neutral PM**, not software-development-locked — the same role system
  should make sense for a marketing initiative or a household project, not just a sprint of code.
- Skill-tree abilities are **tightly coupled** real capability + game-layer effect: every functional
  unlock (e.g. a leveled Facilitator unlocks retro templates; a leveled Adventurer unlocks backlog
  automation) also has a visible game effect (animation, buff, title) — one system, not two parallel
  ones.
- Ember's coaching specializes per the player's currently active role.

## Team formation and backlogs

- A "team" (Party) forms either from **real invited coworkers** or from **in-game matchmaking**
  (guild/party-style grouping with strangers) — both supported, player's choice.
- Gameplay operates on **both** layers simultaneously: each member's individual real tasks still drive
  their personal progress, and the team can additionally create and manage a **shared team backlog**
  together, with team quests layered on top for shared XP/powers.

## SAFe-inspired mapping (Portfolio tier)

- Portfolio epic → Realm objective
- Capability → Campaign capability
- Feature → Major quest
- Story → Quest
- Task → Encounter
- Program Increment → Season

## Portfolio/community tier

Multiple teams coordinate through **both**:

1. A **persistent cross-team dependency board** — async negotiation of shared goals and dependencies.
2. **Scheduled, time-boxed live ceremony events** (PI-Planning-style) — synchronous, with Ember
   facilitating.

This mirrors how real PI Planning actually works: async dependency negotiation leading up to a
synchronous big-room event, not a simplified stand-in for it.

Portfolio-tier roles (Lean Portfolio Management, RTE-equivalent) are:

- **Level-gated** like other roles (a higher-tier, scarcer skill tree players level into through play), and
- **Also community-elected/recognized** — the community must additionally recognize the talent, on top
  of the player being capable. This hybrid meritocracy reflects real organizational scarcity of these
  roles; it is not a pure XP grind.

This mapping and progression must never contaminate the consumer experience with administrative
burden — the dual-label vocabulary and role system should feel like game content, not paperwork, even
as it teaches the real thing underneath.
